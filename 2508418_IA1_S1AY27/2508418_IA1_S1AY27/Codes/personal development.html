Personal Development

My reflection on lessons from The Richest Man in Babylon.

Required Reading

The assignment requires reading “Seven Cures for a Lean Purse” and “Five Laws of Gold” from George S. Clason's The Richest Man in Babylon.

Open the PDF

Seven Cures for a Lean Purse

My Reflection

The chapter “7 Cures for a Lean Purse” taught me that becoming financially responsible begins with developing good habits rather than simply trying to earn more money. The seven cures focus on saving a portion of what I earn, controlling my spending, making my money grow, protecting my savings, preparing for the future, and increasing my ability to earn. One lesson that stood out to me was the importance of saving before spending. This reminded me of my childhood experience when my mother would allow me to save my lunch money and other money I received in a bucket. She encouraged me to leave the money there until I had accumulated a significant amount before spending it. At the time, I saw it mainly as a way of keeping money, but now I understand that she was teaching me the same discipline described in the chapter. The idea of putting aside a portion of my income instead of spending everything I receive is something I can continue applying as a university student.

Five Laws of Gold

My Reflection

The “5 Laws of Gold” further explain how money should be earned, protected, and allowed to grow. The first law emphasizes saving at least a portion of what I earn, while the other laws focus on making money work productively, protecting it through careful decisions, understanding investments before putting money into them, and avoiding unrealistic promises of quick wealth. These lessons showed me that having money is not enough; I also need to have the knowledge and discipline to manage it properly. The laws made me think about how easy it can be to spend money without considering future needs. My experience of saving money in a bucket when I was younger connects strongly to the first law because it taught me that even small amounts can accumulate when I consistently leave some money aside instead of spending it immediately. I can apply the five laws to my life by becoming more careful with the financial decisions I make as I continue my education and prepare for my career. Before putting money into something, I should understand how it works instead of following advice simply because someone says it will make me rich. I also need to avoid being attracted to opportunities that promise quick or unrealistic returns. Instead, I can focus on saving, learning, gaining useful skills, and making informed financial decisions. The biggest takeaway I gained from this chapter is that wealth requires both patience and knowledge. Saving money is the first step, but protecting and using that money wisely is equally important. Overall, the chapter taught me that financial responsibility is a lifelong process, and the habits I develop now can influence my financial future.

Biggest Takeaway

The biggest takeaway I gained from this chapter is that wealth requires both patience and knowledge. Saving money is the first step, but protecting and using that money wisely is equally important.
